\maketitle

\paragraph{The question.} When a character is deleted from a replicated
list, what must the system remember about it, and for how long? The
requirement throughout: once any user has seen character $u$ displayed
before character $v$, no user may ever see $v$ before $u$; and replicas
with the same events must agree.

\paragraph{The cast.} Three characters, identified by \emph{when} they
were typed (larger = later): $d$ at time~1, $a$ at time~4, $x$ at
time~6. Concurrent same-position characters display newest-first, so a
document containing $a$ and $d$ at the same position shows ``$a\ d$''.

\section{The four-event history}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cll@{}}
\toprule
step & action & screen \\
\midrule
1 & type $d$ (time 1) & $d$ \\
2 & type $a$ (time 4), same position & $a\ d$ \\
  & \emph{--- both replicas sync ---} & $a\ d$ \\
3 & \textbf{Alice}: type $x$ (time 6) \emph{after} $d$ & $a\ d\ x$
    \quad$\Rightarrow$ \textbf{Alice has seen $a$ before $x$} \\
4 & \textbf{Bob}: delete $d$ & $a$ \\
5 & \emph{merge} & ? \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent What $M_1$ sees, record by record:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllll@{}}
\toprule
node & in $L$ ($=V_1$) & in $A$ & in $B$ & verdict \\
\midrule
$a$ (4) & root & root & root & in all three $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{lives} \\
$d$ (1) & root & root & \textbf{absent} & live in LCA, missing in $B$
  $\Rightarrow$ $B$ deleted it $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{dies} \\
$x$ (6) & absent & par $= d$ & absent & not in LCA, born in $A$
  $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{lives} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Survivors $\{a, x\}$. The whole question is the root order:
\textbf{$a$ before $x$, or $x$ before $a$?}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
design & root comparison & result \\
\midrule
RGA (keep $d$ as tombstone) & $a$(4) vs $d$(1): $a$ first; $x$ rides
  after $d$; read skips $d$ & $a\ x$\quad\checkmark \\
always keep the dead time & $x$'s rank $=(1,6)$ via dead parent; $4$ vs
  $(1,6)$: $4>1$ & $a\ x$\quad\checkmark \\
keep only if $d$ conflicted & $d$ never conflicted $\Rightarrow$ nothing
  kept; $4$ vs $(6)$: $6>4$ & $x\ a$\quad$\boldsymbol\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The conditional design reverses the order Alice already saw.
The point: \emph{the position of $x$ relative to $a$ is decided by the
timestamp of $d$} --- a character that is gone, and that looked
completely uninteresting at deletion time (typed sequentially, never
part of any conflict). Any rule that skips remembering in ``boring''
cases skips exactly this one. (A second, six-event counterexample kills
the smarter condition ``remember $d$ if something displays above it'':
that condition can change \emph{after} $d$'s death, via a concurrent
insert in a replica where $d$ still exists, so replicas answer it
differently.)

\section{Where the LCA helps, and where it stops helping}

At $M_1$, $d$'s timestamp is still present in two of the three inputs
--- the LCA remembers it. So the \emph{first} merge after a death could
answer correctly with no retention at all, just by consulting its
inputs; the conditional design fails at $M_1$ by rule, not for lack of
information. Retention is forced \emph{one merge later}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[node distance=9mm and 6mm]
  \node[ver] (m1) {$M_1=\{a,\ x\}$ \quad ($d$ is gone from this state)};
  \node[verB, below left=9mm and 4mm of m1]  (p) {$P$: ins $y$(7)};
  \node[verR, below right=9mm and 4mm of m1] (q) {$Q$: ins $z$(8)};
  \node[ver, below=22mm of m1] (m2)
    {$M_2=\mathrm{merge}(L{=}M_1,\ P,\ Q)$:\quad $d$ appears in \textbf{no} input};
  \draw[e] (m1) -- (p); \draw[e] (m1) -- (q);
  \draw[e] (p) -- (m2); \draw[e] (q) -- (m2);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

\noindent $M_2$ must still order $a$ vs $x$, and no input contains $d$.
Whatever $M_1$ learned had to be written into its \emph{output state}.
That is exactly what the working designs do. After $M_1$:
\[
  \text{RGA: } d^{\dagger}\ \text{kept in the sequence}
  \qquad\qquad
  \text{ours: } x : (\mathrm{par}=\mathrm{root},\ \mathrm{rank}=(1,6))
\]
and $M_2$ reads $z\ y\ a\ x$ in both (machine-checked).

\paragraph{Division of labor.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The LCA covers the first merge after a death} --- ``dead
in a branch, live in the LCA'' is visible for free, timestamp included.
This is real power that two-way-merge CRDTs lack.
\item \textbf{The LCA cannot cover the second merge.} Once the death is
processed, the deleted record leaves the visible horizon, and the
ordering fact it justified must survive in the state: as a tombstone
(RGA) or as one retained timestamp on the descendant (ours).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Status of the retention question.} Machine-checked:
remembering nothing breaks (8 refuted designs); remembering
conditionally breaks (2 refuted rules, counterexamples above); always
remembering works and is observationally equivalent to the tombstoned
RGA, at one number per deleted character with living descendants ---
$O(\text{history})$ in the worst case, same as RGA. The one open
question: whether the retained timestamps can be discarded once every
replica has seen the deletion (causal stability), which would shrink the
steady state to the live text plus in-flight activity.
