\author{Sal / FP Launchpad --- KC + Claude}
\date{2026-07-14}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This note gives the complete design of the \emph{delta tree}: a replicated
list whose state is a tree of live nodes carrying \emph{parent-relative}
fractional ranges (deltas), whose delete is an \emph{isometric fold}
(survivor positions arithmetically unchanged), and whose three-way merge is
OR-set survival plus a \emph{local} overlap-splitting repair. The design is
the endpoint of a systematic exploration: every component is forced by a
named, machine-checked countermodel to some alternative. It is the only
candidate in its corner of the design space: \emph{strictly dead-free}
(no dead identifier survives anywhere in the state) while aiming at
convergence and order preservation, with a merge simple enough to mechanize.
We work through the operations and the merge on concrete examples, tabulate
the full litmus battery (L1--L25) against the published tombstoned RGA and
the proved tombstone-free RGA, and state the anomaly contract. We then
report the verdict of randomized property-based testing over a general MRDT
execution model (many replicas, random merges under the LCA discipline):
\textbf{the design is refuted} --- battery-clean, including every named
countermodel that killed its eight predecessors, yet flipping co-displayed
pairs at random-DAG scale (43/120 executions). We extract and minimize the
countermodel (now litmus L25), identify the mechanism (\emph{fold-verdict
erasure}: a dead node's timestamp is load-bearing for repair verdicts it
participated in), refute the one principled repair as well
(\emph{source-consistent folding}, killed by a second mechanism,
\emph{repair non-locality}), and draw the structural conclusion: the three
properties \{strictly dead-free, pairwise display stability, topology
convergence\} now each have a machine-checked witness for every pairwise
combination and eight failed attempts at the triple. What survives of the
design --- relative deltas as a representation layer, and the isometric fold
as stability-gated compaction --- survives \emph{below} an immutable-position
specification, not as a rival to one.
\end{abstract}

\section{The design}

\paragraph{State.} A finite map from timestamps (which double as element
identities) to records
\[
  \sigma : t \;\mapsto\; (\,\mathrm{par},\ (lo, hi),\ \mathrm{char}\,),
  \qquad 0 \le lo < hi \le 1 ,
\]
where $(lo,hi)$ is the node's range \emph{relative to its parent's range} ---
the \emph{delta}. The root sentinel $0$ owns the unit interval and is never
stored. A node's \emph{absolute} range is the affine composition of the
deltas along its root path. The state contains \textbf{live nodes only}, and
--- the design's distinguishing ambition --- \textbf{no dead identifier
persists anywhere}: not as an entry, not as a name inside a field.

\paragraph{Insert.} \emph{Carve}: the new child of anchor $a$ takes a slice
of $a$'s free child-space above the current head --- with remaining relative
gap $(b,1)$ (where $b$ is the current head's $hi$, or $0$), the newcomer
takes $\bigl(b+\tfrac{w}{4},\, b+\tfrac{w}{2}\bigr)$, $w = 1-b$, leaving
room above for future siblings and below for its own children. Newer
siblings sit higher; the display (descending) shows them first --- the RGA
convention, realized geometrically instead of by timestamp comparison at
read time.

\paragraph{The operation payload (per the proved RGA).} Following the
\emph{why-the-path-matters} analysis: $\mathsf{rc}$ must be
\textsf{Either} everywhere (any oriented insert/delete pair awakens the
refuted \texttt{cond\_comm\_base}), so the insert carries its anchor's
ancestor \emph{id-path} plus \textbf{one} absolute slice --- the composed
delta at mint time. Ids suffice for the path because the isometric fold
(next) makes the absolute position invariant under ancestor deletion: a
reordered application resolves the id-path to the nearest live ancestor and
re-relativizes the carried absolute --- landing exactly where
insert-then-delete would have put it. On reachable states the anchor is
live and none of this is read: the path is ghost state in the strict,
machine-checked sense (\texttt{ins\_path\_free}).

\paragraph{Delete: the isometric fold.} Remove the record; re-parent each
child to the grandparent with the dead node's delta composed in:
$c.\mathrm{frac} := d.\mathrm{frac} \circ c.\mathrm{frac}$. Every
survivor's absolute position is unchanged --- \emph{delete-order
preservation is an arithmetic identity}, where the flat RGA's climb
re-sorts (the $[b,a,c]\to[c,b]$ anomaly that started this whole
exploration) and the rose tree's splice needed sixteen thousand lines it
ultimately could not have.

\section{The merge}

$\mathrm{merge}(\lca, A, B)$, $\lca$ the least common ancestor:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Survival} (OR-set): live iff in all three or born in
        exactly one branch.
  \item \textbf{Values}: shared nodes (all in $\lca$, by the LCA
        discipline) take the \emph{LCA's} $(\mathrm{par}, \mathrm{frac})$
        --- the canonical, topology-free base frame, deliberately reverting
        branch-local repairs; branch-born nodes bring their branch's.
        \emph{Why revert?} Branch values encode private merge history;
        computing the repair over them makes the output depend on merge
        order --- the machine-checked failure of global re-ranging (litmus
        L22). The LCA's values are the unique data both branches share.
        Because a repair is a deterministic function of the overlap
        family's union and its members' timestamps, reverting loses
        nothing: the merge \emph{recomputes} the branch's repair,
        canonically extended (\S\ref{sec:refute} tests this claim).
  \item \textbf{Dead-chain folding}: a survivor whose parent is merge-dead
        folds through the dead chain using the LCA's records
        (dead-in-merge $\Rightarrow$ live-in-LCA: the LCA does the
        remembering, as in the proved RGA --- but isometrically, not by
        re-sorting).
  \item \textbf{Local repair} (the overlap split): per parent, in relative
        coordinates, connected components of overlapping child ranges have
        their \emph{union} re-split into equal slots in timestamp order,
        newest topmost; only the members' own fractions are rewritten ---
        descendants are relative and follow automatically ($O(1)$ per
        member: the delta payoff). Singletons untouched; no global
        rebalance; recurse.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Worked example.} Branches carve twins: $\lca$ empty; $A$:
$\ins(10\,{\leftarrow}\,0)$, $B$: $\ins(6\,{\leftarrow}\,0)$ --- identical
computations, identical slices $(\tfrac14,\tfrac12)$. Merge: family
$\{10, 6\}$, union $(\tfrac14,\tfrac12)$, timestamp order $10 > 6$:
$10 \mapsto (\tfrac38, \tfrac12)$, $6 \mapsto (\tfrac14, \tfrac38)$;
display $[10, 6]$. The repair wrote two fractions; had $6$ carried a
subtree, it would have moved with it.

\section{The execution model, and the refutation}
\label{sec:refute}

\paragraph{The model.} Randomized version-DAG executions
(\texttt{litmus/pbt.py}): $n$ replicas; per round each replica either
issues honest operations against its own read or merges with a peer ---
legal only when some recorded version's event set equals the two heads'
intersection (the LCA discipline); final convergence rounds drive all
replicas to the full event set along different merge paths. Checks:
\textbf{FLIP} (global pairwise display stability: across every read of
every replica, no pair is ever shown in both orders), \textbf{CONV}
(equal event sets $\Rightarrow$ equal reads), \textbf{LIVE/DUP}
(survival correctness). 120 executions, 4 replicas, 8 rounds.

\paragraph{Verdict.} \textbf{43 of 120 executions flip a co-displayed
pair.} The battery-clean design fails at random-DAG scale --- in shapes
involving a repair, a delete of a repaired node, and a merge with a branch
that had witnessed the repair's verdict.

\paragraph{The minimized countermodel (now litmus L25).} Five nodes.

The arithmetic of the flip: in the $M_1$ line, the repair left
$10 = (\tfrac38,\tfrac12)$ and $6 = (\tfrac14,\tfrac38)$; $22$'s
\emph{relative} slice under $6$ is $(\tfrac58,\tfrac34)$ (it carved above
$16$). The fold uses the LCA's $6 = (\tfrac14,\tfrac12)$:
\[
  22 \;\mapsto\; \tfrac14 + \tfrac14\cdot(\tfrac58,\tfrac34)
     \;=\; (\tfrac{13}{32}, \tfrac{7}{16})
     \;\subset\; (\tfrac38, \tfrac12) = 10 .
\]
Folded $22$ lands \emph{inside} $10$'s repaired slot; the repair re-splits
the family $\{10, 22\}$ by timestamp --- and $22 > 10$, so $22$ goes on
top: $[22, 10, 16]$, flipping the co-displayed $(10, 22)$.

\paragraph{The mechanism: fold-verdict erasure.} The repair verdict
``$10$ before $6$'' is a fact about the \emph{pair of timestamps}
$(10, 6)$. Strict dead-freedom erases $6$ --- its timestamp with it --- so
$6$'s heir re-litigates the slot with \emph{its own} timestamp, and wins a
contest its parent had lost. The dead node's timestamp was load-bearing
for every order it participated in. This is the nameless-carving lemma
(L20) reaching the fold: pairwise display stability requires the loser's
\emph{name} to persist, in some form, as long as the verdict matters.

\paragraph{The repair candidate, also refuted.} \emph{Source-consistent
folding} --- fold each node through the same input that supplied its value,
so the fold uses the branch's (verdict-bearing) frame. It fixes L25 and
the battery. The DAG PBT kills it too (41/120), by a second mechanism:
\textbf{repair non-locality}. Two concurrent twins $(10, 4)$ are
tie-decided by timestamp at one merge ($[10, 4]$, on a replica whose
history is disjoint from both final inputs); elsewhere, $4$ is re-slotted
by repairs against \emph{unrelated} nodes; at a causally disjoint merge the
pair's geometries no longer tie, and position order --- not the timestamp
verdict --- decides: $[4, 10]$. Re-slotting inside one family perturbs
relations outside it; verdicts encoded in geometry are not stable under
other verdicts.

\section{Conclusions}

\paragraph{The design is refuted, and the refutation is structural.} Eight
variants of the mutation family (scalar keys, nameless ranges, global
re-ranging, local splits with and without normalization, absolute and
relative storage, LCA and source-consistent folds) have now produced eight
machine-checked countermodels, the last two by the two mechanisms above.
The pattern admits a one-sentence summary: \emph{order verdicts must be
re-derivable, identically, at every replica and every time --- and the only
data that guarantees this is immutable data that names the participants,
including the dead ones.}

\paragraph{The triangle.} Every pairwise combination of the three desiderata
now has a machine-checked witness; the triple has eight failed attempts:

\paragraph{What survives.} Two of the design's ideas are correct and
permanent, one level down the stack: \emph{(i)} parent-relative deltas are
the right \emph{representation} of any position tree --- $O(1)$ subtree
transport, structural sharing, lazy absolute sums --- provided the
\emph{semantics} above them is an immutable-position specification
(path-2), whose per-level $(\mathrm{side}, \mathrm{uid})$ data is exactly
the retained name the triangle demands; \emph{(ii)} the isometric fold is
the right \emph{compaction}: sound precisely when gated on causal
stability, where ``everyone has seen the delete'' certifies that no
verdict involving the dead node can ever be contested again --- the same
condition under which the L25 mechanism is disarmed. The delta tree is not
a rival to the immutable-position design; it is its runtime.

\section*{Postscript (same day): the corrected merge --- the design stands}

KC's response to \S\ref{sec:refute}: \emph{``the design is almost right; the
merge is not observing some invariants.''} Correct, twice over. Comparing the
delta tree's and path-2's executions at a flipping PBT trace showed the
state's \emph{identity} data (birth-parent chains) sufficed to reconcile
every order, while the merge was consulting --- and corrupting --- the
\emph{geometric} data instead. Three invariants, violated by every merge in
\S\ref{sec:refute} and observed by the corrected one:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Arbitration from identity only.} Every order decision at a
merge is computed from immutable birth-parent chains (per level: newest
timestamp first; parents before their subtrees) --- never from current
fractions, which repairs perturb (mechanism 2), and never through
frame-mixed folds (mechanism 1). The chains subsume both mechanisms: a dead
node's timestamp persists in its descendants' chains, so verdicts it
participated in remain derivable.
\item \textbf{Geometry is a rendering.} The merge re-derives all fractions,
realizing the canonical order; reads remain purely geometric and never
consult the chains.
\item \textbf{Render/carve compatibility.} The re-render reproduces
sequential-carve geometry (oldest lowest, quarter slices, headroom above),
making post-merge states indistinguishable from sequentially carved ones ---
the residual failures of an intermediate fix were zero-width slices minted
after a render that filled a parent's space completely.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Verdict (machine-checked)}: the corrected design ---
\emph{KC's delta tree as the runtime (unchanged local operations: carve,
isometric fold; geometric reads), with a birth-parent ledger as the
merge-time arbitration substrate} --- is \textbf{clean on the full battery}
(except one-sided L19, as every one-sided design) \textbf{and on the DAG PBT
at 120/120 and 300/300} (6 replicas, 12 rounds). The triangle stands ---
the ledger retains dead identifiers at live-reachable scope, so the design
is not \emph{strictly} dead-free --- but the retention is the minimum the
triangle demands: one parent id per node, no slot data, no materialized
paths, nothing consulted at read time. The delta tree and the path are not
rivals but layers: geometry for reading, identity for deciding.
