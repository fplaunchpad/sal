\section{Proof Strategy}\label{sec:proof-strategy}

The proof follows the manuscript's three steps: the \emph{Eight Conditions},
the \emph{Join Lemma}, and \emph{Canonical Correctness of Every Reachable
Configuration}. The checked argument retains these steps but makes the
representation of event metadata an explicit obligation. It first proves
that a merge represents the union of its inputs, then transfers that result
to canonical replay, and finally lifts it through executions. The independent
sequential specification is addressed in Section~\ref{sec:history-bridge}.

\subsection{The Eight Conditions}\label{sec:vcs}
The manuscript counts three replay laws and five merge equations. The three
replay laws (noncommutation agreement with $\rcrel$, absence of an
$\rcrel$ chain, and conditional commutation in the presence of an absorber)
are stated in the checked guarded form by the globally guarded laws of Definition~\ref{def:guards};
the invariant-scoped route has its own replay and policy obligations.
The join induction below does not silently infer metadata reconstruction from
these three laws. It consumes the five equations together with the explicit
representation and replay supply stated below. In particular, an acyclic
semantic order alone is insufficient for its choice of the last event.

\paragraph{Abbreviations.}
Write $\F{do}(s,e)$ for the effect of the original event $e$ and
$\F{merge}(l,a,b)$ for three-way merge. The context $C$ supplies the events
and visibility of the configuration; it is held fixed during the join proof.
Use the raw set-relative order $\loOn{E}$ of
Definition~\ref{def:order}, with all-state concrete commutation $\comm$.
Its absorber is searched for in $E$, so the order is recomputed after an event
is removed. The invariant-scoped history order has its separately stated
commutation test; it is not substituted into the raw join induction.

\begin{definition}[Indexed concrete representation]\label{def:representation}
For each event context $C$, a representation is a predicate $R_C(E,s)$
on event sets $E$ and concrete states $s\in\Sigma$. An event context records
$\mathcal E_C$ and its visibility relation, as supplied by a configuration.
We write $R(C,E,s)$ interchangeably with $R_C(E,s)$.
The assertion $R(C,E,s)$ relates the \emph{entire concrete state} $s$ to a
specific event set $E$ in context $C$. It need not be defined as reachability,
and it is not inferred from the desired query answer. Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf{Supp}_C(E)&\iff E\subseteq\mathcal E_C,\\
 \mathsf{Unique}(R)&\iff
   \forall C,E,s,t.\ R(C,E,s)\land R(C,E,t)\Longrightarrow s=t.
\end{aligned}
\]
A history is causally closed when
\[
 \mathsf{CausalClosed}_C(E)\iff
 \forall a,b.\ a\vis b\land b\in E\Longrightarrow a\in E.
\]
An enumeration $\pi\equiv E$ is a finite list without repetitions whose
members are exactly $E$. Thus every finite-set assumption below is an
explicit enumeration assumption.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Why the reconstruction past changes.}
The manuscript uses the principal downset of noncommuting visibility.
A concrete representation may need a commuting predecessor's identifier
or anchor to reconstruct an event. Accordingly the checked contract chooses
an explicit dependency relation $M_C$, and writes
$\downarrow_M e$ for its principal downset. Commutation is the concrete relation $\comm$. All representation equations
below use concrete state equality.

\begin{definition}[Metadata dependencies]\label{def:metadata}
For every context $C$, choose a relation $M_C$ on events satisfying
\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_C(a,b)&\Longrightarrow a\vis b,\\
 a\vis b\land\neg(a\comm b)
    &\Longrightarrow M_C(a,b).
\end{aligned}
\]
It may retain additional visible predecessors which commute. Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf{Closed}_{M_C}(E)&\iff
   \forall a,b.\ M_C(a,b)\land b\in E\Longrightarrow a\in E,\\
 \downarrow_M e&=\{x\mid x=e\ \lor\ M_C^+(x,e)\},\\
 \mathsf{Max}^{\F{lo}}_{C,E}(e)&\iff
   \forall x\in E\setminus\{e\}.\ \neg\F{lo}^C_E(e,x),\\
 \mathsf{Max}^{M}_{C,E}(e)&\iff
   \forall x\in E\setminus\{e\}.\ \neg M_C(e,x).
\end{aligned}
\]
The two maximality conditions are separate. If $E\subseteq U$ is
$M_C$-closed and $e$ is $M_C$-maximal in $U$, then
$E\setminus\{e\}$ remains $M_C$-closed. Semantic maximality alone does not
justify this step. For the embedded sequence and anchored queue ports,
$M_C=\visw$; their reconstruction past retains all causal metadata.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Five concrete merge VCs]\label{def:merge-vcs}
The following conditions are universally quantified over the displayed
contexts, histories, events and states. Define the common hypotheses
\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_C(E)&:=\mathsf{Supp}_C(E)\land\mathsf{Closed}_{M_C}(E),\\
 \mathsf{Admissible}_C(E_1,E_2,e)&:=
   \mathsf{Trans}(\visw)\land\mathsf{Irrefl}(\visw)
   \land A_C(E_1)\land A_C(E_2)\\
 &\hspace{12mm}\land\mathsf{Max}^{\F{lo}}_{C,E_1\cup E_2}(e)
   \land\mathsf{Max}^{M}_{C,E_1\cup E_2}(e).
\end{aligned}
\]
Within each condition, abbreviate $R_C(E,s):=R(C,E,s)$,
$D:=\downarrow_M e$ and $E^-:=E\setminus\{e\}$.
These abbreviations compress only repeated hypotheses.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{VC\arabic*.},leftmargin=*,itemsep=.65em]
\item \emph{Merge commutativity.} From
\[
 A_C(E_1),\ A_C(E_2),\ R_C(E_1\cap E_2,l),\ R_C(E_1,a),\ R_C(E_2,b)
\]
conclude $\F{merge}(l,a,b)=\F{merge}(l,b,a)$. This equation is required on represented
triples. The manuscript asks for the stronger equation on arbitrary states;
that stronger equation discharges this restricted obligation.

\item \emph{Initial branch.} From $A_C(E)$ and $R_C(E,s)$ conclude
\[
 \F{merge}(\sigma_0,\sigma_0,s)=s.
\]
This closes the induction when one branch and its intersection are empty.

\item \emph{Local redistribution.} Assume $\mathsf{Admissible}_C(E_1,E_2,e)$,
$e\in E_1\setminus E_2$, and \emph{all} the representation premises
\[
\begin{gathered}
 R_C(E_1\cap E_2,l),\quad R_C(D^-,B),\quad
 R_C(E_1^-,t),\quad R_C(E_2,b),\\
 R_C(D,\F{do}(B,e)),\quad R_C(E_1,\F{merge}(B,t,\F{do}(B,e))),\\
 R_C((E_1\cup E_2)^-,\F{merge}(l,t,b)).
\end{gathered}
\]
Then
\[
 \F{merge}(l,\F{merge}(B,t,\F{do}(B,e)),b)
 =\F{merge}(B,\F{merge}(l,t,b),\F{do}(B,e)).
\]
The event occurs in one branch only. The equation moves its effect from
inside the reconstructed branch to outside the merge of the remaining events.

\item \emph{Shared redistribution.} Assume $\mathsf{Admissible}_C(E_1,E_2,e)$,
$e\in E_1\cap E_2$, and
\[
\begin{gathered}
 R_C((E_1\cap E_2)^-,t_0),\quad R_C(D^-,B),\\
 R_C(E_1^-,t_1),\quad R_C(E_2^-,t_2),\quad R_C(D,\F{do}(B,e)),\\
 R_C(E_1\cap E_2,\F{merge}(B,t_0,\F{do}(B,e))),\\
 R_C(E_1,\F{merge}(B,t_1,\F{do}(B,e))),\quad
 R_C(E_2,\F{merge}(B,t_2,\F{do}(B,e))),\\
 R_C((E_1\cup E_2)^-,\F{merge}(t_0,t_1,t_2)).
\end{gathered}
\]
Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\F{merge}\bigl(\F{merge}(B,t_0,\F{do}(B,e)),\\
 &\hspace{12mm}\F{merge}(B,t_1,\F{do}(B,e)),\F{merge}(B,t_2,\F{do}(B,e))\bigr)\\
 &\hspace{20mm}=\F{merge}\bigl(B,\F{merge}(t_0,t_1,t_2),\F{do}(B,e)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The event occurs in both branches and their intersection. The equation
moves the three reconstructed occurrences outside the merge, leaving one.

\item \emph{Causal delta.} Assume transitive, irreflexive visibility,
$A_C(U)$, $e\in U$, both $\mathsf{Max}^{\F{lo}}_{C,U}(e)$ and
$\mathsf{Max}^{M}_{C,U}(e)$, and
\[
 R_C(U^-,s),\quad R_C(D^-,B),\quad R_C(D,\F{do}(B,e)),\quad R_C(U,\F{do}(s,e)).
\]
Then $\F{merge}(B,s,\F{do}(B,e))=\F{do}(s,e)$.
After redistribution this equation removes the last merge: the event is
applied once to the represented remainder.
\end{enumerate}
The represented smaller-union merge in VC3 and VC4 is obtained by a
\emph{strictly smaller} induction call. Its presence is not a premise
asserting the desired join theorem for the original union. The represented
side reconstructions are also derived on proper subsets, or by VC5 when a
side equals the whole union.
\end{definition}

\subsection*{Reusable verification templates}
\label{sec:verification-templates}
The five equations are a reusable interface, rather than five independent
proof scripts for each datatype. A template fixes a family of implementation
algebras and proves that a declarative instance of that family supplies
Definition~\ref{def:merge-vcs}. In the checked interface, an instance retains
the datatype $D$, public operation policy $P$, concrete representation $R$
and metadata dependencies $M$. Its soundness statement has the shape
\[
 \mathsf{Input}(D,P,R,M)\Longrightarrow\mathsf{MergeVCs}(P,R,M).
\]
The conclusion contains exactly the five equations and their represented-state
premises. The instance cannot replace $R$ by a weaker query relation or replace
$P$ or $M$ by a convenient alternative.

The reusable families include commuting updates with empty reconstruction past,
policy-controlled replay, immutable certified records, ordered records,
products with monotone components, and ordered records with an archive.
A query extension preserves the update and merge algebra; its transport rule
therefore preserves the five VCs. This transport alone proves no assertion
about the new query's independent sequential specification.

The datatype author chooses the family and identifies its implementation maps.
For the ordinary and efficient OR-sets, proof-free annotations record the event
comparison and, optionally, the record type and carrier/update/birth/kill maps.
The frontend selects witness or mask replay, constructs the templates, and
proves their finite laws from registered definitions. Embedded and Sided RGA identify
eight ordered-record maps, raw-equation annotations and chain/code mappings;
generic list, comparator and prefix-code laws
construct their finite kits from checked raw recursive equations, and the frontend
projects their existing issuance and replay contracts. Anchored Queue and
Peritext reuse this route, including the Sided Core and RichCore transports.
For Fugue, an archive template derives the finite kit from live-list and
archive projection equations. Compositional prefix-code laws derive coordinate
injection, while generic issuance lemmas propagate record validity and creator
evidence. The datatype still supplies its code specialization, issuer lookup
facts and the connection to its unchanged issuance check. Other families still supply their finite collection
of algebraic laws and evidence projections explicitly. Here \emph{finite} describes the number of obligations,
not a finite test domain: each law is universally quantified over its typed
inputs. Evidence projections may concern every represented history. The
annotations do not define the independent sequential specification or prove
its correctness bridge.

The verification command applies the generic soundness theorem and searches
registered declarative instances. Its registration interface rejects completed
VC and join contracts as inputs. Missing family laws remain typed proof goals;
there is no fallback to an existing datatype VC theorem. The resulting proof
term is checked by Lean. Automation thus packages a proved algebraic argument;
it does not infer correctness from executions sampled by a test harness.

To obtain the Join Lemma, the author still supplies uniqueness, initial
representation, finite enumeration, and replay supply as stated below.
To obtain RA-linearizability, the independent sequential bridge in
Section~\ref{sec:history-bridge} remains necessary. In particular, applying an
ordered-record template to Fugue's implementation leaves the
sequential-specification obstruction of Example~\ref{ex:fugue} unresolved.

\begin{definition}[Replay supply and a peel witness]\label{def:replay-supply}
At a fixed context $C$, replay supply consists of both clauses:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=.25em]
\item For every $E$ and $\pi$, if $\pi\equiv E$ and
$\mathsf{Supp}_C(E)$, some $s$ satisfies $R_C(E,s)$.
\item For every represented, supported, nonempty, $M_C$-closed $U$, there
exist $e\in U$ and states $s,B$ satisfying
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \mathsf{Max}^{\F{lo}}_{C,U}(e),\qquad\mathsf{Max}^{M}_{C,U}(e),\\
 R_C(U^-,s),\quad R_C((\downarrow_M e)\setminus\{e\},B),\\
 R_C(\downarrow_M e,\F{do}(B,e)),\quad R_C(U,\F{do}(s,e)).
\end{gathered}
\]
\end{enumerate}
The second clause is the \emph{peel witness}. It supplies the represented predecessor state and the represented effect of
appending the original event both to its metadata past and to the remainder.
It does not assume that merging preserves representation. Its existence is a
separate datatype obligation; acyclicity of $\F{lo}^C_U$ alone supplies neither
metadata maximality nor the four representation assertions.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The Join Lemma}\label{sec:join}

The manuscript's canonical-state equation becomes a representation statement
until the correspondence with canonical replay has been proved.

\begin{theorem}[Join: finite concrete representation induction]\label{thm:join}
Assume the five VCs, $\mathsf{Unique}(R)$, and
\[
\begin{aligned}
 &\forall C,E,s.\ R(C,E,s)\Longrightarrow R(C,\varnothing,\sigma_0),\\
 &\forall C,E,s.\ R(C,E,s)\Longrightarrow\exists\pi.\ \pi\equiv E.
\end{aligned}
\]
Fix a context $C$ with transitive, irreflexive visibility and replay supply.
For all $E_1,E_2,l,a,b$, if $A_C(E_1)$, $A_C(E_2)$ and
\[
 R_C(E_1\cap E_2,l),\qquad R_C(E_1,a),\qquad R_C(E_2,b),
\]
then
\[
 R_C(E_1\cup E_2,\F{merge}(l,a,b)).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof structure]
Induct strongly on the length of a duplicate-free enumeration of
$E_1\cup E_2$. An empty side uses uniqueness, the initial-representation
hypothesis and VC2, with VC1 if the right side is empty. Otherwise replay
supply chooses a peel witness. Removing its event preserves metadata closure
by $M_C$-maximality. Supply gives represented predecessor states for the
branches and their intersection. The smaller induction calls establish
side reconstruction and the smaller-union merge representation. Uniqueness
identifies the reconstructed sides with the original sides; VC3 or VC4
then brings the event outside. VC5 and the peel witness's represented update
reconstruct the union. Every recursive call has a strictly smaller event
set; none assumes representation preservation of an unresolved merge.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The full-causal-closure join contract]
The checked derived contract quantifies over fully causally closed
$E_1,E_2$, not over all merely conflict-closed histories. To obtain it from
Theorem~\ref{thm:join}, require replay supply for every $C,E_1,E_2,a,b$
with transitive, irreflexive visibility, supported sides and
$R_C(E_1,a),R_C(E_2,b)$. This is the quantifier order of the supply hypothesis
in the checked derived theorem. Causal closure implies $M_C$-closure because
$M_C\subseteq\visw$. Hence the theorem gives the concrete
\emph{representation join} contract
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \mathsf{Supp}_C(E_1),\ \mathsf{Supp}_C(E_2),\quad
 \mathsf{CausalClosed}_C(E_1),\ \mathsf{CausalClosed}_C(E_2),\\
 R_C(E_1\cap E_2,l),\ R_C(E_1,a),\ R_C(E_2,b)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad R_C(E_1\cup E_2,\F{merge}(l,a,b)),
\end{gathered}
\]
under transitive, irreflexive visibility. This contract includes intermediate
representation evidence which canonical query agreement by itself does not
provide.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Canonical Correctness of Every Reachable Configuration}\label{sec:adequacy}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical correctness of certified executions]\label{thm:execution}
Fix an internal replay policy whose directed event relation is $\rho$.
This auxiliary policy supplies concrete replay witnesses; it is separate from
the public payload policy and from the revised invariant-scoped history order.
Let $K_\rho(a,b):=\rho(a,b)\lor\rho(b,a)$. Its set-relative order is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \F{lo}^{\rho,C}_E(a,b)\iff{}&
 (a\vis b\land K_\rho(a,b))\\
 &\lor\bigl(\neg(a\vis b)\land\neg(b\vis a)\land\rho(a,b)\\
 &\hspace{12mm}\land\neg\exists c\in E.\ b\vis c\land K_\rho(b,c)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Define
\[
 \mathsf{IsCan}^{\rho}_C(E,s)\iff
 \exists\pi.\ \pi\equiv E\land
 \mathsf{Respects}(\pi,\F{lo}^{\rho,C}_E)\land
 \pi(\sigma_0)=s.
\]
Suppose, for \emph{every} configuration $C$ satisfying
original mint honesty, the following full-causal-closure canonical join
contract holds:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \mathsf{Trans}(\visw),\ \mathsf{Irrefl}(\visw),\quad
 \mathsf{Supp}_C(E_1),\ \mathsf{Supp}_C(E_2),\\
 \mathsf{CausalClosed}_C(E_1),\ \mathsf{CausalClosed}_C(E_2),\\
 \mathsf{IsCan}^{\rho}_C(E_1\cap E_2,l),\quad
 \mathsf{IsCan}^{\rho}_C(E_1,a),\quad\mathsf{IsCan}^{\rho}_C(E_2,b)\\
 \Longrightarrow\quad\mathsf{IsCan}^{\rho}_C(E_1\cup E_2,\F{merge}(l,a,b)).
\end{gathered}
\]
Every ordinary or recursive-virtual issuance-certified execution from the
initial configuration then has $\mathsf{IsCan}^{\rho}_C(E,s)$ at every allocated
version $C.S(v)=(s,E)$. This theorem concludes existence of a respecting replay. To recover the
manuscript's canonical-state equality, additionally establish uniqueness of
such folds under the internal policy; write their common state as
$\F{can}^{\rho}_C(E)$; then the join contract reads
$\F{can}^{\rho}_C(E_1\cup E_2)=
\F{merge}(\F{can}^{\rho}_C(E_1\cap E_2),
\F{can}^{\rho}_C(E_1),\F{can}^{\rho}_C(E_2))$.
The execution theorem itself needs only the displayed witness contract.
Its event sets are supported and causally closed;
visibility is transitive and irreflexive. Moreover, if the store invariant and
this canonical configuration invariant hold, its recursive virtual base for
versions $(s_1,E_1)$ and $(s_2,E_2)$ is canonical for $E_1\cap E_2$.
\end{theorem}

To derive the displayed join hypothesis from the datatype equations, the five VCs, uniqueness, initial representation, finite enumeration
and replay supply yield representation join. A port additionally proves
\[
 R_C(E,s)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathsf{IsCan}^{\rho}_C(E,s)
\]
for supported, causally closed histories in each mint-honest context
with transitive, irreflexive visibility. The backward implication makes the
three canonical inputs represented; representation join constructs the union;
the forward implication makes its result canonical. The embedded ports have
exactly this correspondence. Other ports can use stronger represented
canonical-state certificates rather than asserting this equivalence generally.

The execution induction carries the store invariant together with canonicality.
The initial version replays the empty list to $\sigma_0$. A query preserves
that invariant and a fork copies it. An application appends its freshly
issued event to a replay of the previous head; the new event has no successor
in the resulting event set, so this list respects its set-relative order.
The merge cases use the canonical join contract.
Ordinary merges use the actual greatest common ancestor's intersection history.
This event-intersection coherence is an admissibility property of the
configuration in Definition~\ref{def:execution}; for operational stores it is
proved from the store invariant, rather than from graph ancestry alone.
Recursive virtual merges use a nested induction on finite ancestry support:
the inner fold represents the union of its inputs, and each recursively
computed base represents their intersection. Original issuance guards remain
at original mint states. Neither intermediate peeling nor scratch computation
requires reissuing an event. This theorem is a merge-and-replay guarantee;
legality and specification visibility of one independent sequential word are
additional obligations of the final correctness bridge.
