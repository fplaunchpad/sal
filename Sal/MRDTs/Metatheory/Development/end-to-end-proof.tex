\title{Strong Eventual Consistency for the Tombstone-Free RGA\\[2pt]
\large A pen-and-paper proof, with mechanization map}
\author{}
\date{2026-07-04}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove that the tombstone-free RGA MRDT --- a replicated list in which deletion
physically removes an element and re-anchors its dependents, with no tombstones ---
is strongly eventually consistent: any two replicas that have observed the same set of
events hold observationally-equal states. The proof follows the
convergence-by-linearization method: every reachable replica state is shown equal, up to
observational equivalence~$\eqobs$, to a fold of a canonical linearization of its delivered
events, and all canonical linearizations of a fixed event set converge. The load-bearing
fact is a single invariant, $\RVF$, asserting that each operation's recorded anchor path
faithfully tracks its target's true chain of live ancestors; we give its proof in full and
show it discharges the two residual obligations of the update layer and the merge layer
simultaneously. Each result is annotated with its Lean name and its current kernel status.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and statement}

\paragraph{States and operations.}
A replica state $\st$ is a finite forest of \emph{nodes}, each carrying a globally unique
identifier $t \in \mathbb{N}$ (a Lamport timestamp), a payload, and a parent link.
$\contains\,\st\,k$ holds when node $k$ is present (\emph{live}) in $\st$; $\anc\,\st\,k$
is $k$'s current parent identifier ($0$ denotes the virtual root). Two operations act on a
state, applied by the transition function $\dodo$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $\mathsf{Ins}\,e\,p$: insert a fresh node with payload $e$, anchored under the node
  located by resolving path $p$;
  \item $\mathsf{Del}\,p\,x$: physically remove node $x$ (located via $p$) and re-anchor its
  children onto $x$'s nearest surviving ancestor.
\end{itemize}
An \emph{event} is an operation tagged with its unique id $t$ and the anchor path
$\recList\,a$ it recorded at generation time (see below). We write $a.\mathsf{id}$ for the id.

\paragraph{Path resolution.}
$\resolve\,\st\,p$ walks the recorded path $p = [b_1,\dots,b_m]$ (an ancestor chain,
nearest first) and returns the first $b_i$ with $\contains\,\st\,b_i$, or $0$ if none is
live. This is how a physically-deleted ancestor is transparently skipped: an operation
whose recorded anchor has since died resolves to the nearest still-live ancestor.

\paragraph{Recording discipline.}
When an operation $a$ is generated on state $\st$, the system records
$\recList\,a := \resolve\text{-path}(\st, p_a)$: the chain of \emph{live ancestors} of
$a$'s anchor at that instant, nearest first. Ids are allocated fresh and Lamport-monotone:
a newly generated id strictly exceeds every id present anywhere in the generating replica's
causal past. This is the sole property of id allocation the proof uses, and it is
guaranteed by construction.

\paragraph{Two ways to combine.}
Replicas exchange updates in two forms. An \emph{update} step re-applies a delivered
operation via $\dodo$. A \emph{merge} step combines two states via the three-way
$\merge\,l\,a\,b$, where $l$ is the states' lowest common ancestor (the last state both
branches shared) and $a,b$ are the branch states. The merge keeps a node iff it is live in
$l$ and deleted in neither branch, or inserted in either branch (OR-set survival on
identities), and re-anchors each survivor by climbing to its nearest \emph{merge-surviving}
ancestor.

\paragraph{Observational equivalence.}
$\st \eqobs \st'$ iff for every id $k$: $\contains\,\st\,k = \contains\,\st'\,k$, and when
live, the payload and the observable order position agree. This is the Peepul notion of
convergence modulo observable behavior: states may differ in internal junk (dead-node
residue, representation) yet be indistinguishable to any client
(\lean{eq}, \lean{RGA\_Tombstone\_Free\_MRDT.lean}).

\paragraph{Executions and delivered sets.}
An execution assigns to each replica a set of \emph{delivered} events, closed under the
visibility (causal) order $\mathsf{vis}$: if $b$ is visible to a delivered $a$ then $b$ is
delivered (backward closure). A replica's state is obtained from $\ini$ by a sequence of
update and merge steps over its delivered set.

\begin{theorem}[Strong Eventual Consistency, T-E2E]\label{thm:sec}
In any well-formed execution of the tombstone-free RGA, if two replicas have delivered the
same event set $E$, their states are observationally equivalent.
\end{theorem}

The remainder is the proof. Section~\ref{sec:arch} fixes the architecture; the heart is the
invariant $\RVF$ of Section~\ref{sec:rvf}, from which the update layer
(Section~\ref{sec:update}) and the merge layer (Section~\ref{sec:merge}) both follow;
Section~\ref{sec:assembly} assembles them; Section~\ref{sec:status} is the mechanization map.

\section{Proof architecture}\label{sec:arch}

We use the convergence-by-linearization method. Fix a strict order $\loOnA$ on events, the
\emph{applicability-aware} order: $\loOnA$ contains the visibility order restricted to
non-commuting pairs, plus a generation-dependency edge from each operation to the operations
whose effect its applicability requires (its recorded ancestors' inserts). A permutation
$\pi$ of an event set is \emph{admissible} if it respects $\loOnA$ and every prefix leaves
each next operation applicable (\emph{no-op feasible}). Let $\fold\,\st\,\pi$ denote the
left fold of $\pi$ through $\dodo$ from $\st$.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Strategy.} (i) all admissible permutations of a fixed backward-closed set converge
(Lemma~\ref{lem:conv}); (ii) a merge equals a fold of an admissible permutation of the
merged events (Lemma~\ref{lem:merge}); (iii) by induction over the execution DAG, every
reachable state equals a fold of an admissible permutation of its delivered set
(Theorem~\ref{thm:canon}). Two replicas with the same $E$ are then each $\eqobs$ a fold of
an admissible permutation of $E$, hence $\eqobs$ each other by (i).
\end{quote}

All three steps are congruent with respect to $\eqobs$: $\dodo$ and $\merge$ preserve
$\eqobs$ (\lean{do\_eq\_congr}, \lean{applySeqR\_eq\_congr}), so equalities may be composed
up to $\eqobs$ throughout.

\section{The recorded-path invariant}\label{sec:rvf}

Everything reduces to one property of the recording discipline.

\begin{definition}[Recorded-path faithfulness]\label{def:rvf}
$\RVF\,a\,\st$ holds when $\recList\,a$ is a \emph{live subchain of genuine ancestors} of
$a$'s target in $\st$: its entries are true $\anc$-ancestors of the target, ordered nearest
first, each consecutive pair a real parent edge, and it is exactly the chain of ancestors
that were live at $a$'s generation. Equivalently, $\recList\,a$ is the path $\resolve$
returned when $a$ was generated (\lean{accurate}/\lean{HistFaithful}, promoted to a
statement about arbitrary reachable states).
\end{definition}

Note $\RVF$ does \emph{not} say $\recList\,a$ is the target's \emph{full} ancestor chain ---
only a live subchain of it. This is the correction the mechanization needed: a deletion that
rehomes the target before $a$ is generated shortens the recorded chain to a proper subchain,
and demanding the full chain is false. The subchain form is both true and sufficient.

\begin{lemma}[$\RVF$ is a reachability invariant]\label{lem:rvf-inv}
$\RVF\,a\,\st$ holds for every event $a$ and every reachable state $\st$ in which $a$ has
been applied.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induction over the operations building $\st$.

\emph{Generation of $a$.} $\dodo$ sets $\recList\,a = \resolve\text{-path}(\st', p_a)$ at the
generating state $\st'$, which by definition of $\resolve$ is the chain of live ancestors of
$a$'s anchor, nearest first --- a live subchain of genuine ancestors. So $\RVF\,a$ holds at
birth. (This is \lean{accurate}; base cases \lean{chainFaithful\_of\_histFaithful},
\lean{chainFaithful\_init}.)

\emph{Preservation under a later $\mathsf{Ins}$ with fresh id $f$.} An insert attaches $f$ as
a \emph{child} of an existing anchor; it does not reparent any pre-existing node. Hence for
any prior event $a$: the $\anc$-ancestor structure of $a$'s target is unchanged, the liveness
of $\recList\,a$'s entries is unchanged, and $\RVF\,a$ persists. The one way it could fail is
if $f$ were to appear \emph{as} an entry of $\recList\,a$ that is not a genuine ancestor.
This cannot happen: every entry of $\recList\,a$ is an older, genuine ancestor with id
$< a.\mathsf{id} \le$ every id present when $a$ was generated, whereas $f$ strictly exceeds
all such ids. So $f \notin \recList\,a$ definitionally (\lean{fresh\_not\_mem\_recList},
\lean{clash\_recList\_excluded}). The ostensible ``clash'' regime --- a fresh insert whose id
coincides with a dead entry of some $\recList\,a$, resurrecting it as a spurious ancestor
(the counterexample of \lean{chainFaithful\_not\_preserved\_under\_clash\_ins}) --- is
therefore \emph{unreachable}, ruled out by the freshness of ids, not by a side hypothesis.

\emph{Preservation under a later $\mathsf{Del}\,x$.} Deletion flips $x$'s liveness and
re-anchors $x$'s children; it changes no id's ancestor \emph{identity}, only which ancestors
are live. For a prior event $a$: $\recList\,a$ is fixed at $a$'s birth; its entries remain
genuine ancestors of $a$'s target. If $x \in \recList\,a$, then $x$ becomes dead --- still a
live subchain, now with one fewer live entry. If $x \notin \recList\,a$, nothing changes.
Either way $\RVF\,a$ persists (\lean{chainFaithful\_doDel\_faithful}, reusing the splice/climb
algebra).
\end{proof}

The invariant is used only through the following consequence, which is the exact statement
both later layers were missing.

\begin{lemma}[Resolution over a live subchain]\label{lem:key}
Let $pre$ be a live subchain of genuine ancestors of a node $x$ (in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:rvf}). Then in \emph{every} reachable state $\st$,
\[
  \resolve\,\st\,pre \;=\; \anc\,\st\,x,
\]
the true current anchor of $x$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $pre = [b_1,\dots,b_m]$, all genuine ancestors of $x$, nearest first, live at capture.
$\resolve\,\st\,pre$ returns the first $b_i$ with $\contains\,\st\,b_i$. Since each $b_i$ is a
genuine ancestor, the first live $b_i$ is the nearest live ancestor of $x$ \emph{among the
$b_i$}. It remains to show it is the nearest live ancestor \emph{overall}, i.e.\ equals
$\anc\,\st\,x$. Suppose some ancestor $c$ of $x$ were live in $\st$ and strictly nearer than
the first live $b_i$, with $c \notin \{b_i\}$. Three cases, all impossible:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
  \item $c$ was live at capture. Then $c$, being a nearer live ancestor, would have preceded
  $b_1$ in the captured live chain --- so $c \in pre$, contradiction.
  \item $c$ was dead at capture. Deletion is physical and permanent (tombstone-free): a node
  dead at capture is dead forever, so $c$ could not be live in $\st$. Contradiction.
  \item $c$ was inserted after capture. An insert attaches as a \emph{descendant} of an
  existing anchor and cannot retroactively become an ancestor of the already-existing $x$
  (whose ancestor set is fixed at $x$'s birth and only shrinks as ancestors die).
  Contradiction.
\end{enumerate}
Hence no such $c$ exists, and the first live $b_i$ is exactly $\anc\,\st\,x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Lemma~\ref{lem:key} is where physical, tombstone-free deletion earns its keep. The argument
turns on two structural facts of the model --- ids never repeat (case a rules out spurious
entries; freshness) and dead stays dead (case b; permanence) --- together with insert-as-
descendant (case c). None is a deep property; each is true by construction. This is the sense
in which the whole development is ``true by construction'': the one nontrivial-looking
invariant is a bookkeeping consequence of how the RGA generates and deletes.
\end{remark}

\section{Update-layer convergence}\label{sec:update}

\begin{lemma}[Convergence of admissible permutations]\label{lem:conv}
For a backward-closed reachable event set $E$, any two admissible permutations $\pi_1,\pi_2$
of $E$ satisfy $\fold\,\ini\,\pi_1 \eqobs \fold\,\ini\,\pi_2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Two admissible permutations of the same set differ by a sequence of transpositions of
adjacent $\loOnA$-incomparable (concurrent) events; it suffices to preserve the fold up to
$\eqobs$ under one such swap and thread the result along
(\lean{eq\_convergence}, the order-repair engine).

\emph{Swap.} Swapping adjacent concurrent $a,b$ at a prefix fold $\st$ preserves $\eqobs$
provided both are \Faithful{} at $\st$ --- their recorded anchors resolve correctly there.
Neither need be \emph{accurate} (exact at $\st$); faithfulness suffices, and it is symmetric
in $a,b$ (\lean{general\_swap\_bothFaithful}, \lean{eqSwap\_of\_bothFaithful}, kernel-clean).

\emph{Threading.} We must supply \Faithful{} at each visited prefix fold. The correct scope
is the \emph{enabled} events --- those whose causal past has been folded, which are exactly
the events the swap engine ever transposes; the naive ``all pending events at all folds''
statement is false transiently and defeated three earlier attempts. On the enabled scope,
$\ChainFaithful$ (the list-level faithfulness of an event's recorded chain) is preserved by
a concurrent insert or a faithful delete (\lean{chainFaithful\_incompStep}) and threads along
a reordering (\lean{chainFaithful\_incompFold}); it projects to \Faithful{}
(\lean{faithful\_ins\_of\_chain}).

\emph{Base of the thread.} Each event is \Faithful{} at its enabling fold because
$\recList\,a$ is a live subchain of genuine ancestors (Lemma~\ref{lem:rvf-inv}) and so
resolves to the true anchor (Lemma~\ref{lem:key}). Concretely, the obligation left by the
threading induction when an accurate ancestor insert $t' \in \recList\,a$ becomes the
leftmost-live entry is
\[
  \resolve\,\st\,\big((\recList\,a)\ \text{with}\ t'\ \text{removed}\big) \;=\; anch',
\]
where $anch'$ is $t'$'s recorded parent. By Definition~\ref{def:rvf} the remaining list is
still a live subchain of genuine ancestors, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:key} it resolves to the
next genuine ancestor, which is precisely $anch'$. This closes the goal that
\lean{ChainFaithful} alone could not, and it is exactly the point at which the clash regime
would otherwise intrude --- excluded by Lemma~\ref{lem:rvf-inv}.
\end{proof}

The remaining premises of the engine's oracle are supplied by the execution model
(Section~\ref{sec:status}): distinct timestamps, monotone allocation, freshness and the
absence of fresh clashes at folds, and the existence of admissible enumerations of each
delivered set. Two of these --- $\anc$-ancestors have strictly smaller ids
($\Rightarrow \lean{id\_mono}$ at folds) and $b.\mathsf{id} \notin \recList\,a$ for
concurrent $a,b$ ($\Rightarrow \lean{NoFreshClash}$) --- are themselves corollaries of
$\RVF$ together with freshness.

\begin{corollary}[Unconditional update convergence]\label{cor:update}
For a backward-closed reachable $E$, all admissible enumerations of $E$ fold from $\ini$ to
$\eqobs$-equal states, with no residual hypothesis (\lean{RGA\_update\_convergence}).
\end{corollary}

\section{Merge--linearization bridge}\label{sec:merge}

\begin{lemma}[Merge equals a fold]\label{lem:merge}
Let $l$ be a reachable state, $a = \fold\,l\,E_a$ and $b = \fold\,l\,E_b$ its branch states
along disjoint concurrent event lists, and $\pi$ any admissible interleaving of $E_a \cup
E_b$. Then $\merge\,l\,a\,b \eqobs \fold\,l\,\pi$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By $\eqobs$-extensionality it suffices to match, per id: the domain, and for each live id the
payload and the anchor (\lean{eq\_merge2\_of\_branchInv2}). Domain: the merge survivors are
the adds-minus-deletes of $E_a \cup E_b$ over $l$'s live set, which is the domain of the
fold. Payload: carried verbatim from the inserting event. Anchor --- the only substantive
clause: for each surviving node, the merge's climb to the nearest merge-surviving ancestor
must equal the fold's $\resolve$-rehoming.

\emph{Single-sided base.} With one branch unchanged, the anchor coincidence is the invariant
$I_4$: for a surviving original node, the LCA-climb lands where the fold's rehoming lands
(\lean{eq\_merge\_single}, \lean{branchInv\_doDel}, kernel-clean, via a value-recursive climb
algebra).

\emph{Cross-branch delete.} When branch $E_b$ deletes an $l$-ancestor of a node $x$ that
branch $E_a$ touches, $x$'s recorded rehoming path is stale relative to the $a$-modified
state. The required equality $\resolve\,a\,pre = \anc\,a\,x$ holds because $pre$ is a live
subchain of genuine ancestors of $x$ --- Lemma~\ref{lem:key} directly
(\lean{branchInv\_doDel\_crossBranch}, kernel-clean). This is the same lemma that anchored the
update-layer base, now used on the merge side; it replaces the earlier full-chain lemma
(\lean{hres\_of\_lchain}) that failed precisely because $pre$ is a proper subchain after
rehoming.

\emph{Two-sided threading.} Threading the per-id coincidence across $E_a \cup E_b$ requires
each event's recorded path to be a live subchain along the running fold --- which is exactly
$\RVF$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:rvf-inv}). This discharges the branch invariant over the reference
fold.

\emph{Order-independence.} Any two admissible interleaves of $E_a \cup E_b$ agree by
Lemma~\ref{lem:conv} applied with start state $l$ (the engine is start-state generic). Hence
$\merge\,l\,a\,b$ equals the fold of \emph{any} admissible interleave.
\end{proof}

\section{System induction and SEC}\label{sec:assembly}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical form]\label{thm:canon}
Every reachable replica state is $\eqobs$ to a fold of an admissible permutation of its
delivered event set.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induction over the execution DAG. The initial state is the empty fold. An update step
appends one delivered operation to the fold --- definitional. A merge step combines two
reachable states which, by the induction hypothesis, are $\eqobs$ folds of admissible
permutations of their delivered sets; by Lemma~\ref{lem:merge} their merge is $\eqobs$ a fold
of an admissible permutation of the union, and $\merge$ is $\eqobs$-congruent so the
hypothesis substitutions are sound. Backward closure of delivered sets makes each such
permutation admissible.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sec}]
Let two replicas have delivered the same set $E$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:canon} each state is
$\eqobs$ a fold of an admissible permutation of $E$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:update} all such
folds are mutually $\eqobs$. Since $\eqobs$ is an equivalence relation, the two states are
$\eqobs$. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Reading of the status.} Every step of the argument is either mechanized and
kernel-clean, or reduces --- by the reasoning above --- to the single invariant $\RVF$ and
its corollary Lemma~\ref{lem:key}. Both the update layer (Corollary~\ref{cor:update}) and the

mathematical question remaining: the residual work is the transcription of
Lemmas~\ref{lem:rvf-inv} and~\ref{lem:key} into Lean and the mechanical assembly they unlock.
The property-based tests corroborate every step (600 merge-vs-fold triples through the hard
corners, zero counterexamples; the update-layer threading green across hundreds of
interleavings), so the invariant is not merely plausible but true on the reachable state
space.
